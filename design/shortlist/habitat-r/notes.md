# habitat-r — four paints for a settled framing

Habitat's framing is locked: a thing plus exactly one piece of the world it belongs to. Its
rendering never was. The incumbent is a modelled solid with a generic key light, inherited from
clay rather than chosen for this job.

Habitat differs from every other depiction in a way that should decide the paint: it already
contains light and ground. A sun beside the Earth, a glow under the fire, a shadow under the
person, a ripple under the drop. That is a lighting setup. So three of these treat light as the
system rather than as a per-item trick.

The context is the same element in every column; only what it is made of changes.

What the render settles:

- **golden hour is undermined by something habitat does not have: a floor.** Its whole device
  is one low sun so every shadow falls the same way, but habitat's ground is drawn per subject
  — a line here, a ripple there, nothing at all for Star or Calculus — so there is no continuous
  surface for a cast shadow to land on, and on a dark card the shadow is invisible. The warm
  and cool split still reads. The shared shadow does not. Fixing it means giving every subject
  a floor, which is a change to the framing and not to the paint.
- **wash is the most distinct and answers a complaint the others cannot.** A piece of world
  behind a sprite always looks pasted on; running both through the same bleed makes the ground
  and the person one pigment, and the edge between them is where the water dried.
- **shadow box works and sits close to the incumbent.** Flat colour plus a real cast shadow is
  a small move away from a modelled solid, and at 34px it is nearly the same picture.
