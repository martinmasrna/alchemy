# abstracts — the eight with nowhere to be

The style is settled and the next job is thirty-one icons. This is not the twenty-three easy
ones: it is the eight that might break the settled style, taken first on purpose.

Habitat's rule is a thing plus exactly one piece of the world it belongs to, and it was decided
on subjects that live somewhere — a star among stars, a fire on its logs, a car on a road, a
person on a ground line. World 1 is the world before anywhere exists. Energy, Matter, Space,
Time, Particle, Light, Gravity and Lightyear have no habitat, and four of them are the seeds,
so they are the first icons anyone sees. If the rule cannot hold them, the problem is the style
decision and not the drawing.

Three answers, same silhouettes, same paint. Only the piece of world changes.

- **effect** — the world-piece is what the abstraction does to something else. Light gets the
  floor it lands on, Gravity the sheet it dents, Lightyear the two stars it spans, Time a scale
  of moments, Energy the ground it struck. Faithful to the rule, and eight separate inventions.
- **void** — all eight share one world-piece, the early universe as a cold scatter of first
  light held to the margins. Drawn once. It also gives world 1 an arc the player can see: these
  float in the dark, and ground only arrives with Rock, Lava and Ocean.
- **bare** — no world at all, and the absence is the signal.

## What the drawings settled on the way

Energy was radial rays first, which is the monoline's idea and the truer one — energy is what
happens, not what is. In amber at 34px it was a second Star, and Energy is a seed the player
meets ten discoveries before Star exists. The bolt is a worse idea that survives the grid.

Space came out better than expected. A piece of dark, cut out and lifted, with the stars on the
front sheet rather than behind it, is the most literal thing shadow box can do and it reads.

Gravity is the case for effect on its own. Without the dented sheet it is a grey ball with four
arrows, and the sheet is the only drawing on the page where the world does the explaining.

Lightyear reads as a dumbbell in every column except effect, where the two stars rescue it.
