# habitat-x — the depiction separated from the paint

The final three were not answering the same question. Editorial and glass are both answers to
how a thing is rendered. Habitat answers what else is in the frame, and its own rendering is
a modelled solid that happens to look like clay. So habitat was never really competing with
the other two, and the choice between them was a false one.

Here the depiction is held and only the paint changes. The context is byte-for-byte identical
across the three columns: the same stars beside the star, the same ripple under the drop, the
same sun beside the Earth, the same long shadow under the person, the same road under the car.

One thing has to change with the ground rather than the paint, and it is worth remembering:
**context has to be drawn in whatever ink the surface takes.** A glow under a fire says nothing
on paper, so the editorial column's fire keeps its logs and loses its halo, and the star's
white companions become dark specks. A piece of world is not a sprite you can move between
backgrounds.

What the render settles:

- **habitat as glass and habitat as it won are close relatives.** Both are dark, both model the
  form with light. Glass adds a facet seam and a harder specular; that is the whole difference,
  and at 34px it nearly vanishes. If the choice is between those two it is a small choice.
- **habitat as editorial is the genuinely different one**, and it is the only one of the three
  that turns the grid into a field of pale tiles.
- **the context survives all three renderings**, which is the useful result: the depiction is
  portable, so it is not tied to whichever paint wins.
