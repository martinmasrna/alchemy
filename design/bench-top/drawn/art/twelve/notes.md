# twelve — round 9 (the set that actually decides it)

Martin's twelve: Star, Water, Earth, Life, Human, Fire, Hunting, Tribe, City, Car, Calculus,
Bitcoin. Certain to be in the game, and spanning the whole diamond rather than the prologue.
It is a far harsher test than six cosmology subjects, because half of it is scenes with people
in them and two of them have no physical appearance at all.

Locked: expected colour where a thing has one, the layout, the dark chrome, 34px in a card.
Free: the art style.

Both of my predictions about cosmic were wrong, in opposite directions.

- I said it could not draw a cell. It draws Life fine, and Fire and Water beautifully.
- I said it survived the last round. It does not survive this one. **Human is a dark shape
  with a glow behind it and is unreadable without the label.** Hunting, Tribe, City and Car
  go murky at 34px. The system is one thing lit against darkness, and half of this game's
  subjects do not emit light. Six cosmology subjects flattered it; twelve real ones do not.

The other three all clear all twelve.

- **screenprint** is the only one where a scene is native, and it is the most atmospheric.
  Cost: every icon is a small framed picture, so the grid reads as twelve tiny posters rather
  than twelve things.
- **harper** is the most readable at 34px by a distance, and the reduction does carry wit.
  Calculus is bars under a curve, which is literally how the integral was invented.
- **editorial** has the best answers to the two abstractions and to Human, and the grain gives
  it warmth the other two flat styles lack.

One thing that applies to all three and is bigger than the icons: they each put a pale tile
behind every subject. On a near-black screen that turns the grid into a field of light
squares, which changes the whole screen rather than just its icons. Choosing any of them is
also choosing that, or choosing to solve it.
