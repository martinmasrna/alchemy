# round14 — glass and wax won, so four relationships between form and light

Felt stops light dead, crystal bounces it off flat planes, and both lost. Glass and wax both
let light into the material and give it back changed. The property being selected for is
translucency and internal light.

That is two narrowing rounds running, and glass and wax already converge at 34px, so four
grades of glow would have repeated the sub-class problem. Instead all four are translucent and
lit from within, and the varied thing is structural:

- **lantern** — the form is an opaque shell with the light inside it. The only column where the
  body itself is dark; light escapes at the rim and throws a halo onto the card.
- **liquid** — no surface anywhere. Form exists only as a gradient of concentration.
- **gel** — almost entirely surface. A taut bright rim, speculars sitting on top rather than
  inside, and a body you can faintly see the card through.
- **plasma** — the light is the material. Brightest at the boundary, thin through the middle.

What the render settles:

- **liquid fails in the grid.** At 72px it is the most atmospheric thing in the whole search;
  at 34px Star, Life and Tribe are unreadable blurs. This is the medium working as designed
  rather than a bug: a thing with no surface has no silhouette, and a grid is nothing but
  silhouettes. It is the clearest case yet of a style that is beautiful and cannot do the job.
- **lantern and plasma converge at 34px.** Both reduce to a dark body with a bright outline,
  and the difference between escaping light and emitted light disappears at that size.
- **gel survives the shrink best**, because it is the only one of the four with a hard edge.
