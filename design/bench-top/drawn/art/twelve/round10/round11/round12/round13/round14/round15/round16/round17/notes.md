# round17 — habitat advanced, so four kinds of world

Habitat's idea is that a thing gets exactly one piece of the world it belongs to. Its weakness
was that the piece defaulted to a ground line, four times out of six. So the idea is held and
the kind of world is varied: around, upon, beside, behind.

The six objects are drawn once and shared by all four columns, so context is the only variable.

- **medium** — what the thing is immersed in, as a field around it. Underwater, in dust, in a
  shaft of hazy air, inside its own smoke, inside a network.
- **light** — no scenery at all. Place given entirely by where the light comes from.
- **neighbour** — one companion, so the context is a relationship rather than a place.
- **horizon** — a distant edge behind, so the world is depth rather than ground.

What the render settles:

- **neighbour is the only column that keeps the grid tile-free.** Everything floats on the
  card, which is the thing Martin objected to back in round 11 and the only column here that
  respects it. It is also the most legible at 34px.
- **light produces the single best image in the round** (Human backlit at dusk) and the single
  weakest (Star, where a self-lit thing lit by itself says nothing new).
- **horizon is the most uniform.** Every card is a sky and an edge, so the six read as a set of
  stamps. That is coherence and sameness at once, and which one it is depends on whether the
  grid should feel like a collection or like a place.
- **medium is the least consistent**, because four of six have an obvious surrounding field
  and two do not: a star in dust and a coin in a network are both nearly invisible additions.
