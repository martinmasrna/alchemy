# round19 — the last one

Eighteen rounds of taxonomy ran out of road, so this is four ideas rather than four points on
an axis.

- **constellation** — the oldest thing humans ever did with a sky: join unrelated points of
  light and insist they are a bull, a hunter, a drop of water. It is also exactly what the
  player does all session, looking at things with nothing to do with each other and deciding
  they go together. The only direction in nineteen rounds that is *about* the game.
- **mural** — one continuous painting, every icon a window cut into it. Colour runs off all
  four edges because it belongs to something larger, so the grid is a shattered fresco rather
  than a shelf.
- **relief** — nothing is drawn. Everything is pressed into a surface and a raking light does
  the work. Colour is a pigment rub settled in the recesses.
- **reflection** — every thing comes with its own still water. The grid goes quiet, every
  colour is stated twice, and the horizon sits at the same height on all thirty cards.

What the render settles:

- **constellation is the only one that survives being tiny**, because a bright dot on black is
  the highest-contrast mark available. It is also the only one whose Bitcoin is legible as a
  constellation rather than as a coin, which is either charming or a failure depending on
  whether the player is meant to recognise or to decode.
- **relief needs two presses.** A single uniform press loses every interior, so Earth was a
  green disc and Bitcoin a plain orange one. Interiors have to be pressed deeper than the body,
  which is also true of the real thing.
- **mural is the only direction whose value is invisible in a single tile.** It only does its
  job in the grid, where the colour fields fail to line up across the gaps and the whole thing
  reads as one broken surface.
- **reflection is the calmest and the most repetitive.** The same horizontal line at the same
  height on every card is order at small doses and wallpaper at thirty.
