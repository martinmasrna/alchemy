# round18 — back to the depiction axis (first of the final two)

Round 17 was a framing mistake, mine: habitat advanced and I varied habitat's sub-kinds rather
than staying on the axis, which is the round 12 narrowing error in new clothes. All four came
out worse than the thing they were meant to improve.

Four depiction strategies not previously tried:

- **scale** — the thing beside something whose size everyone already knows.
- **plan** — everything from directly overhead, one unusual viewpoint held across the set.
- **specimen** — the thing collected, mounted and catalogued, on a plinth with a number rather
  than in a frame, because a frame gives every icon the same silhouette and that mistake has
  been made twice.
- **macro** — a characteristic detail at huge magnification, never the whole object.

What the render settles:

- **plan splits cleanly on symmetry.** Fire as a ring of stones round a bright centre and
  Human as a head with shoulders are both better than any front view of them in eighteen
  rounds. Star and Bitcoin are radially symmetric, so overhead gives back exactly the front
  view for one and an unreadable ellipse for the other. A viewpoint that only works on
  asymmetric things cannot carry thirty items.
- **specimen is the most consistent thing in the round** and the only one that says out loud
  what the game is, which is a collection of thirty-one things. The plinth reads at 34px and
  the catalogue number does not, which is fine: it is texture at that size.
- **scale loses its reference at 34px.** The tiny Earth beside the star, the match beside the
  flame, the moon beside the planet all vanish, and what is left is the object alone. The
  whole point evaporates at the size where the game is actually played.
- **macro fails on recognition, as predicted.** The iris reads as an eye rather than a human,
  and the milled coin edge reads as stripes. Earth's coastline and the star's granulated
  surface are the two that work, and both are things people have seen photographs of.
