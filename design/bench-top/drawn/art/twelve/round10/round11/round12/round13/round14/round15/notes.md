# round15 — a different axis

Fourteen rounds and about forty candidates all varied one thing: how the object is rendered.
That axis is mined out, which is why each round returned less. What had never been questioned
is the depiction itself. Every candidate so far was a picture of the object, alone, from the
front, because round one assumed it and nothing since argued.

Held: expected colour, the twelve subjects, both sizes, no support unless earned.
Varied: what kind of depiction this is.

- **personified** — the thing has a face and a temperament.
- **typographic** — the thing is its name, and the drawing steps out of the way.
- **cutaway** — the thing opened up, because the inside is the interesting part.
- **naive** — drawn by a hand with no skill, on purpose. Not round four's sketch, which was a
  neat line pretending to wobble.

What the render settles:

- **typographic has a structural problem, not a taste one.** Every card already prints the
  name underneath. A word icon says the same thing twice and leaves the tile carrying no
  information at all. It also dies at 34px, where Calculus and Bitcoin become grey mush.
- **personified survives the shrink better than anything in several rounds.** Two eyes are two
  high-contrast dots, and those read at any size. It is also the only column where the grid
  has a mood.
- **cutaway is the most interesting and the least consistent.** Tribe as a plan view of people
  round a fire is the best single drawing in the whole search; Star reduces to a wedge taken
  out of a disc and reads as a pac-man.
- **naive muddies at 34px.** The wax texture that gives it warmth at 72px turns Car, City and
  Bitcoin into smudges.

Redundancy worth remembering beyond this round: the card already shows the name, so any
direction whose job is to say the name is doing work the layout has done.
