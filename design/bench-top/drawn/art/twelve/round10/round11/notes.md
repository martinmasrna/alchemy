# round11 — oil and inkwash won, so four physical media

Both winners are a tool, a liquid and pigment behaving the way pigment behaves. Both rejects
are systematic textures a machine makes, a dot grid and a gradient with noise, neither of
which carries the mark of anything. Read as: hand and material over system.

Second constraint, and it is the more useful one: **a support has to earn its place.** A
near-black tile on an already dark card is just a darker square doing nothing. A tile is
allowed here only when the medium implies one, or when it is derived from the subject.

- **gouache** — no support at all. Opaque paint hides what is under it, so there is nothing to
  show and the icon sits straight on the card.
- **woodcut** — the sheet is drawn, because a print is ink sitting on paper. Each ink is pulled
  separately and lands slightly off the last.
- **pastel** — the ground is the subject's own dark tone pulled most of the way to a warm grey,
  which is how a pastellist picks a sheet. Derived, not invented.
- **fresco** — plaster, because pigment goes into a wall rather than onto it.

What the render settles:

- **gouache is the only column that leaves the grid dark**, and it is the direct answer to the
  complaint: no tile, no problem. Nothing is lost by removing it.
- **woodcut and fresco flip the grid pale**, the same trade inkwash makes.
- **pastel's tiles are all slightly different**, because each is derived from its own subject.
  In the grid that reads as a patchwork of warm tones. It is the most interesting answer to
  the support question and also the least calm.
- **fresco needed two passes.** The first had additive offsets in its earth matrix plus heavy
  erosion, and pale subjects like Human dissolved into the wall entirely.
