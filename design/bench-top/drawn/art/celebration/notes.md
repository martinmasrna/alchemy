# celebration — round 8 (the brief changed at the root)

The game is a celebration of the beauty of the Universe, Nature and consciousness. Humour is
encouraged rather than excluded: the constraint is that the emotion is entirely positive, and
that the player finishes a session thinking life is good. Delight, not doom, and not cynicism.

That disqualifies competent-and-neutral. Glass was clean, and clean is a UI value. Beautiful
is an art value, and here it is the product rather than the decoration on it.

So these four are not rendering techniques. Each is a tradition that exists *because* somebody
wanted to celebrate something.

Locked: expected colour from round 7, the layout, the dark chrome, 34px in a card.
Free: the tradition.

- **haeckel** — Kunstformen der Natur, 1904, a book made to argue that nature is beautiful.
  Fine ink over jewel colour, radial symmetry, obsessive detail. Built on organic form, so
  worlds 2 and 3 are already paid for.
- **stained** — lead line and jewel field, where the colour only exists because light comes
  through it. The oldest technology for celebrating anything, and literally about illumination.
- **illuminated** — gold leaf on pigment, every subject framed in a roundel.
- **cosmic** — the sublime painted straight, no ornament, the thing itself doing the work.

What the render settles:

- **illuminated is dead on a structural ground**, whatever anyone thinks of it. The roundel
  gives every item the same outer silhouette, so at 34px Energy, Star, Nebula, Water and Earth
  are five gold coins with something inside. A grid whose job is telling things apart cannot
  have one shape for everything. It is also the only solemn column, which now reads as the
  wrong emotion rather than a mood.
- **haeckel and stained keep distinct silhouettes** and are the two joyful columns. Ornament
  here reads as delight rather than ceremony.
- **cosmic is beautiful and has no humour in it at all.** It is also the only column that
  plainly cannot draw a cell, which the three-world test makes a real cost rather than a note.
