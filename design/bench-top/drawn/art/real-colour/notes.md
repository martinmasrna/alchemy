# real-colour — round 7 (art style varies, colour does not)

The rule changed, and it is the right rule. Colour is no longer a variable. The airbrush
Earth was a purple planet with a ring, and it was only Earth because a label said so.

Locked: a single shared palette, set to what a person already expects a thing to be. Sea
blue, land green, cloud white, star yellow-white, nebula violet and magenta. Not what a thing
actually is; what someone recognises without being told. Also locked: the layout, the dark
chrome, the 48px box, 34px in a card.
Free: the art style, and nothing else.

The test each column has to pass: if Earth is not obviously Earth, the column failed at its
job rather than chose a mood.

- **cel** — a comic frame. Key line, flat local colour, one hard shadow, one hard highlight.
  The outline is a deep tone of the object's own colour, never black, which is what stops a
  set of cel drawings looking like clip art.
- **gouache** — painted. Edges displaced by noise so none of them is a vector curve, tone laid
  over tone, grain through the whole thing. The only column that does not look like it came
  out of a computer.
- **pixel** — a different medium. Sixteen by sixteen at three pixels a cell, shading by
  dithering, nothing ever soft. Small is its native size rather than a compromise.
- **glass** — carried forward. It led the last round on hard edges, so it is here to be beaten.

What the render settles:

- every Earth now reads as Earth, which is the whole point of the new rule.
- **cel holds the best silhouette at 34px.** The key line does the work a glow cannot.
- **pixel is the only style that gets better as it gets smaller**, and the only one with a real
  problem at discovery size, where the cells become large squares. Charm or defect, but it is
  a decision, not a detail.
- **gouache softens slightly at 34px** but keeps its texture, which no other column has.

Sprites that took two passes: pixel gravity read as an anchor until the dip ran the full
width, and pixel star had a two-by-four white core that read as a bar through the middle.
