# drawn — round 3 (what the genre actually did)

Round 2 was rejected whole. The axis was right (the icons are the palette) but all three
answers were ways of *processing* emoji, and none of them is what the games in this genre did.

What they did, checked rather than recalled:

- **Little Alchemy 1** — Jakub Koziol drew every icon himself.
- **Little Alchemy 2** — he brought in graphic artist Simon Post for an entirely new set.
- **Doodle God** — a commissioned illustration set, one hand.

So the genre's answer to icon chaos is to own the icons. Big hero icons work in those games
precisely because somebody drew all of them.

This direction takes that answer at the cheapest possible grade: line geometry, one 1.5
stroke, one 24px box, one vocabulary of shapes. No shading, no colour of its own. Every mark
is `currentColor`, so the palette finally drives the icons instead of fighting them, and a
selected card or a tier tint costs nothing extra.

For this subject it is also more accurate than emoji. Hydrogen is one, helium is two, carbon
is a hexagon, oxygen is a bonded pair, light is a wave, space is a volume with almost nothing
in it. World 1 is mostly abstract, and emoji are worst exactly there: the shipped game uses a
balloon for both hydrogen and helium, and a black square for space that is invisible on a
dark screen.

Cost: about 30 marks per world, drawn by us, forever. That is the real price and it does not
go away.

Marks that took more than one pass, so nobody re-breaks them: hydrogen and helium have to be
*countable* at 28px, so they are dots inside a shell, not orbiting electrons; three tidy dots
in a box read as a dice face; a curve inside the nebula blob reads as a mouth.
