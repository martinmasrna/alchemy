# tiers — round 2 (palette and type)

Locked: layout, spacing scale, type ramp, content, mechanics.
Free: the whole visual language — ground, plate, accent, icon treatment, type colour and weight.

Measurement that opened the round: the screen carries nine colour tokens, but the accent is
spent five ways (credits, the goal, a picked card, an armed square, the discovery glow), and
next to it sit 31 emoji with 31 unrelated palettes. Colour on this screen is not a decision
anyone made.

The answer here: take the colour away from the emoji and give it back as information. Icons
are drained to grey; the card, its border and its name carry a tier hue — violet for what you
started with, blue for space, green for the planet. The grid becomes a map of how far you have
come, and a glance tells you which half of the world you are working in. Full colour returns
only at a discovery, so colour is the reward rather than the wallpaper.

The goal's Earth keeps its colour on purpose: it is the one thing you are aiming at.

Cost: emoji lose their charm and some go muddy in grey (the balloon, the black hole). Tiers
have to be authored per item in every world table, which is a content job, not a CSS one.
